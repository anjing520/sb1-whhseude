The ocean freight market has been anything but predictable in recent years. From pandemic-era disruptions to the Suez Canal blockage to shifting trade patterns, shippers have had to navigate constant change. Here is our outlook for the remainder of 2026.

## Capacity: Finding a New Equilibrium

After years of volatility, container shipping capacity is settling into a more stable balance. New vessel deliveries have outpaced scrapping, modestly increasing overall fleet capacity. However, this is being offset by:

- Environmental regulations that require slower steaming speeds
- Ongoing route diversions away from conflict-affected regions
- Continued consolidation among major carriers

The net effect is a market that is better supplied than during the pandemic peak, but tighter than the oversupplied years that preceded it.

## Freight Rates: Stabilizing, but Regionally Uneven

The wild rate swings of recent years are moderating. Spot rates on major east-west lanes have settled into a more predictable range, though they remain sensitive to disruptions.

Contract rates, which lag spot markets, are still adjusting. Shippers with long-term contracts signed at peak rates may find themselves above current spot levels — a dynamic worth reviewing at renewal time.

> The key to cost management in 2026 is flexibility. A blended strategy of contract and spot booking typically outperforms either approach alone.

## Trade Lane Shifts

Several structural shifts in trade patterns are reshaping the market:

### Asia–Europe
Ongoing Red Sea diversions have extended transit times and absorbed capacity. Shippers should plan for longer lead times and maintain higher inventory buffers.

### Trans-Pacific
Trade flows between Asia and North America remain strong, with infrastructure investments on both sides improving throughput at major ports.

### Intra-Regional
Short-sea and regional trade is growing faster than long-haul, driven by nearshoring trends and regional trade agreements.

## What Shippers Should Do

### Diversify Your Carrier Portfolio

Relying on a single carrier creates concentration risk. Working with multiple carriers — and multiple modes — gives you options when disruptions hit.

### Lock In Capacity Early

Peak season capacity is not guaranteed. If you have predictable volume, negotiate contracts well in advance of your shipping window.

### Invest in Visibility

When the market is unpredictable, visibility is your best defense. Real-time tracking and predictive analytics let you spot problems early and respond before they become crises.

## Looking Ahead

We expect 2026 to be a year of relative normalization for ocean freight — but "normal" in this industry still means being ready for the unexpected. The shippers who thrive will be those who combine strong carrier relationships, flexible strategies, and the technology to see what is coming.
